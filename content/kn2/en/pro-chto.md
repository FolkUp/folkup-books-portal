Torah scrolls are written without vowels. Consonants alone on parchment: the text stands wide open, read aloud, copied thousands of times, and yet unreadable in itself. The vowels live in the head of whoever knows how to read. The Torah is not alone in this. Knowledge is rarely hidden behind the whole wall; more often it is handed out with one key held back. This book walks three thousand years of that seesaw. Prometheus, who stole not fire but the knack of making it. The Masoretes, whose "correct" vocalization turned out to be the work of human hands and open to dispute. Gutenberg, who started with printed indulgences. Murano, where the secret of glass was guarded by death and saved by defectors. The engineer Robert Kearns, who spent twenty years in court over authorship of a pause in a windshield wiper. Richard Stallman with the ethics of open code, and Aaron Swartz, who wanted not to break the lock but to cultivate the reader. By the end, the book turns the whole argument on its head: today no one hides the source, they drown it in the flood, and the surest lock hangs not on the door but in the head of whoever stands before it. It ends with Socrates, who wrote not a line. Read this and check yourself: what consonants lie in front of you every day, and whose vowels are you filling in.

— Comandante FolkUp
